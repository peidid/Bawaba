# Qatar health-sector law and MoPH rules on hospital patient information and its disclosure (external AI tool, insurers, MoPH reporting)

Scope note: Qatar only, current to the extent findable as of 22 Nov 2026. The official Al Meezan portal (almeezan.qa) and most moph.gov.qa pages could not be machine-read during this research (JavaScript app / bot challenge), so several article texts come from reputable secondary copies (an unofficial English Penal Code translation, a Weill Cornell Medicine-Qatar legal seminar deck that quotes the statute, law-firm briefings, official news agencies). Each finding below says where it came from. **Arabic text is the authoritative version of Qatari legislation; English translations are unofficial** — [Springer, Medical Liability in Qatar](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-4855-7_13). The general data protection law (Law No. 13 of 2016, PDPPL) is only cross-referenced here; it is covered by other research notes.

Confidence key used below: **[V]** = article text seen in the statute or a verbatim copy of it; **[S]** = reported by a credible secondary source, article text not seen; **[U]** = unverified / conflicting.

## 1. Medical confidentiality / professional secrecy: which laws oblige doctors and facilities to keep patient information secret, and what exceptions allow disclosure?

### Takeaway
Three sets of rules impose medical secrecy in Qatar: Article 20 of Law No. 2 of 1983 (practice of medicine), which carries disciplinary sanctions; Article 332 of the Penal Code (Law No. 11 of 2004), which makes it a crime to disclose a professional secret "in conditions other than those prescribed by the law" without the patient's consent; and the confidentiality duties in Law No. 22 of 2021 and its 2022 Executive Regulations. All three let disclosure happen only with consent or where a law authorises it, such as notifiable-disease reporting, a judicial or criminal process, or a specific statutory data duty. None of them creates a general "legitimate interest" exception that would cover sharing with a commercial AI vendor. I found **no Qatari "medical liability law No. 8 of 2021"**: that citation looks wrong.

### Cited Findings
**Law No. 2 of 1983 on the Practice of the Profession of Medicine and Dental Medicine and Surgery**
- Status: dated 22/02/1983, 30 articles, listed as **in force** on Al Meezan — [Al Meezan, Law No. 2 of 1983](https://almeezan.qa/LawView.aspx?opt=&LawID=253&language=en) (search-result metadata; page body not machine-readable).
- **Article 20 [V via quoted copy]:** a doctor is prohibited from "disclosing confidential information about a patient which he discovered on account of his profession, exempt in those cases permitted by law" — [Holmes, "Legal & Ethical Challenges of Confidentiality in Medical Practice", Weill Cornell Medicine-Qatar seminar deck, Nov 2016](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353).
- **Article 23 [V via quoted copy]:** the standing committee for licenses "may review violations alleged or suspected to have been committed by doctors in breach of the provisions of Article 20" and charges of breaching professional ethics. The deck lists the disciplinary sanctions as a warning, suspension from practice for up to 1 year, or withdrawal of the licence and deletion from the register of doctors — [Holmes deck](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353).
- **Article 18 [U]:** a search snippet says Art. 18 sets out when a doctor is and is not liable for poor outcomes (not liable if due care was given; liable for negligence). I did not see the text — [Al Meezan article page (snippet only)](https://almeezan.qa/LawArticles.aspx?LawArticleID=9091&LawId=253&language=en).
- The medical practice law lists specific prohibited acts and errors but does not define physicians' criminal liability as such — [Al-Sharq op-ed, 21 Dec 2021 (Arabic)](https://al-sharq.com/opinion/21/12/2021/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A4%D9%88%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B7%D8%B1%D9%8A).

**Penal Code, Law No. 11 of 2004** (unofficial English translation)
- **Article 332 [V]:** "Whoever, knowingly and illegally divulges a secret entrusted thereto in his official capacity, trade, profession in conditions other than those prescribed by the law or uses it for his personal benefit or for the benefit of another person, without the consent of the person concerned with the secret, shall be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years in prison and/or a fine of no more ten thousand Riyals (QR 10.000)." — [Penal Code Law 11/2004, English (AUC CMRS copy)](https://www4.aucegypt.edu/CMRS/Files/2.2%20Penal%20Code%2011%202004_EN.pdf). The same penalty is quoted in Arabic commentary — [Al Raya, 16 May 2026](https://www.raya.com/2026/05/16/همسات-قانونية-جريمة-إفشاء-الأسرار-الوظيفية/amp) — and in a trade-secrets review, which miscites the code as "Law No. 11 of 2005" — [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowelltradesecretstrends.com/?p=3171).
- **Article 331 [V]:** spreading news, photographs or comments about a person's or family's private life, "even if true", is punishable by up to 1 year and/or QR 5,000. **Article 333 [V]:** illegal intrusion into private life (opening correspondence, eavesdropping, recording or transmitting private conversations, photographing in a private place) is punishable by up to 1 year and/or QR 5,000 — [Penal Code English copy](https://www4.aucegypt.edu/CMRS/Files/2.2%20Penal%20Code%2011%202004_EN.pdf). These sit in Chapter Four, "Calumny, Defamation and Secret Disclosure (326-333)" (same source).
- **Article 333 bis [S]:** added by Law No. 14 of 2024. It targets broadcasting or unauthorised use of recordings. I have not verified the text — [Al Raya 2026](https://www.raya.com/2026/05/16/همسات-قانونية-جريمة-إفشاء-الأسرار-الوظيفية/amp).
- **Article 186 [V] (a duty that can override secrecy):** anyone who knows in advance that an offence will be committed, could prevent it, and fails without valid excuse to inform the authorities faces up to 3 years and/or QR 10,000. The perpetrator's spouse, ascendants and descendants are exempt — [Penal Code English copy](https://www4.aucegypt.edu/CMRS/Files/2.2%20Penal%20Code%2011%202004_EN.pdf). WCM-Q guidance: a doctor who learns of a crime about to be committed should report directly to police. If the crime has already happened, the doctor reports to MoPH, which decides whether to inform police — [Holmes deck](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353).

**Recognised exceptions (as taught locally; no single statutory list found)**
- Disclosure without consent may be justified where it is (i) required by law, (ii) in the interest of the patient or others, or (iii) in the public interest. "Obligated under the law" examples: criminal investigations, court or tribunal testimony, and infectious-disease rules requiring notification of "notifiable" diseases to public-health authorities. When disclosure is necessary, give the "minimum information necessary to avoid harm". The duty of confidentiality continues after death — [Holmes deck](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353).
- The same deck says Qatar law has special requirements for highly confidential categories: HIV, genetic testing, alcohol and drug treatment, and mental health. It does not cite instruments — [Holmes deck](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353).
- An academic chapter maps who in Qatar's health system is bound by confidentiality, which information counts as secret, the exceptions, and special cases (genetics, COVID-19, emergencies) — [Solaiman, "Data protection, privacy, and confidentiality in Qatar's health system", Elgar 2023](https://www.elgaronline.com/abstract/book/9781035309436/book-part-9781035309436-12.xml); [HBKU record](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/data-protection-privacy-and-confidentiality-in-qatars-health-syst/) (full text not accessed).

**Law No. 22 of 2021 regulating healthcare services within the State (confidentiality limb)**
- Healthcare providers must protect "the confidentiality of beneficiaries' information and data". The source gives no article number — [Al Marzouqi Law commentary](https://malmarzouqilaw.com/commentary-on-law-no-22-of-2021-regulating-health-care-services/).
- The Executive Regulations list "الإخلال بضوابط ومتطلبات سرية البيانات والمعلومات الخاصة بالمستفيدين ومقدمي الرعاية الصحية" (breach of the controls and requirements on confidentiality of beneficiaries' and providers' data) as a violation — [Al Meezan, MoPH Decision No. 8 of 2022 (Arabic), search snippet](https://www.almeezan.qa/LawView.aspx?opt=&LawID=8980&language=ar).

**"Medical liability law No. 8 of 2021"**
- Searches found no Qatari medical liability statute numbered 8 of 2021, and no new medical-liability law issued 2021–2026. Civil and criminal malpractice liability still rests on Law No. 2 of 1983, the Civil Code and the Penal Code (e.g., Arts. 312–313 on negligent harm, doubled where the act breaches the offender's profession) — [Holmes deck quoting Penal Code Arts. 312-313](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353); [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-4855-7_13). An undated op-ed refers to efforts to "activate" a medical liability law with compulsory malpractice insurance, so treat this as possibly pending — [Al-Qarar Al-Arabi op-ed](https://alqraralaraby.com/news/237521/).

### Inferences
- For a hospital, sending identifiable patient data to an **external AI diagnostic vendor** is a disclosure to a third party. Under Art. 20 (Law 2/1983) and Art. 332 (Penal Code) that needs either the patient's consent or a legal basis, and I found no Qatari statute giving AI vendors a legal basis. Practical routes are consent; treating the vendor strictly as a processor or agent of the treating team under contract (which Qatari case law has not tested as falling outside "disclosure"); or sending de-identified data.
- Art. 332 bites on the individual who divulges. The facility's exposure comes mainly through Law 22/2021 violations, licensing, and the PDPPL. Policies should therefore name individual accountability (DPO, clinician, IT) as well as institutional controls.
- Notifiable-disease reporting, court orders, police investigations and MoPH statutory requests are the clearest "permitted by law" exceptions.

### Gaps
- Official Arabic text of Law 2/1983 Arts. 18, 20 and 23 not seen directly (Al Meezan not machine-readable). English wording comes from a WCM-Q legal seminar deck.
- Could not confirm whether the 1983 law has been amended or replaced by a newer practice-of-medicine law. Al Meezan still listed it as in force.
- Commercial guides attribute patient confidentiality to a "Healthcare Practitioners Law, Law No. 12 of 2013" and the QCHP Emiri Decree No. 7 of 2013 ([QCHP Emiri Decree PDF](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa/en/Documents/QCHP%20Emiri%20Decree%20%20(eng).pdf)). I did not verify their confidentiality content. A QCHP/DHP Code of Professional Conduct likely contains a confidentiality clause, but I did not retrieve it.
- No statutory source found for the "special categories" (HIV, genetic, mental health) mentioned in the WCM-Q deck. Qatar's Mental Health Law (Law No. 16 of 2016) was not checked.

## 2. Patient rights: MoPH patients' rights charter, consent requirements, and access to medical records

### Takeaway
Patient rights in Qatar are set out mainly in charters, not statute: the MoPH/DHP "Patients' Bill of Rights & Responsibilities" and HMC's Patient and Family Bill of Rights. Both promise confidential records, read only by those involved in care, and access to or copies of one's own record on request. That access is subject to a medical-inadvisability (physician restriction) exception and to legal prohibitions. Law No. 22 of 2021 empowers the Minister to issue rules on patients' rights and duties.

### Cited Findings
- The MoPH Department of Healthcare Professions (DHP) booklet "Protecting the public and promoting high standards of practice" contains a Patients' Bill of Rights & Responsibilities. It includes a right to request copies of medical records and reports, subject to an exception where release is medically inadvisable — [MoPH/DHP booklet](https://sehanafsia.moph.gov.qa/English/Resources/Documents/Bill%20of%20Rights-Booklet-ENG-AW_LR.pdf) (via search summary).
- HMC charter: patients may "have, upon request, access to all information contained in their medical records, unless access is specifically restricted by the attending physician for medical reasons or is prohibited by law". All records about their care are treated as confidential, and only those directly involved in care should read the record — [HMC Patient and Family Bill of Rights and Responsibilities](https://hamad.qa/EN/Patient-Information/Patient-Family-Rights/Patient-and-Family-Bill-of-Rights-and-Responsibilities/Pages/default.aspx); [HMC poster, 2015](https://www.hamad.qa/en/patient-information/documents/patient%20and%20family%20rights%20poster%20english_2015.pdf).
- MoPH health-rights advocacy (2024) describes the same principle: records are read only by people with a need to know — [The Peninsula, 8 Apr 2024](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/08/04/2024/moph-advocates-for-health-rights).
- Unresolved complaints go to facility management, then to MoPH's Government Health Communications Center or the DHP Fitness to Practice unit — [MoPH/DHP booklet](https://sehanafsia.moph.gov.qa/English/Resources/Documents/Bill%20of%20Rights-Booklet-ENG-AW_LR.pdf).
- Law No. 22 of 2021 leaves further rules on patients' rights and duties, and on categories of entitled persons, to Ministerial decisions and regulations — [Al Marzouqi Law commentary](https://malmarzouqilaw.com/commentary-on-law-no-22-of-2021-regulating-health-care-services/).
- **Consent for storing health information:** MoPH's insurance-data directive states that "Entities are required to get patient consent for all stored protected health information" — [MoPH, Qatar National Health Insurance Data Management Directive v1.0, 20 Dec 2022, §4](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Telemedicine consent:** the DHP telemedicine circular requires patients to give informed consent that covers the limitations and risks of remote care — [DHP circular (Eng)](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa/en/QCHPCirculars/Eng-Circular.pdf) (via search snippet).

### Inferences
- A hospital policy should treat the physician-restriction exception and "prohibited by law" as the only grounds to refuse a patient's own record request. Patient-portal and AI-output disclosures should follow the same logic.
- Because the charters are mostly non-statutory, the enforceable hooks are Law 22/2021 (confidentiality violation), licensing and fitness-to-practice, Penal Code Art. 332, and the PDPPL.

### Gaps
- Could not open the full MoPH charter text, so its numbering and exact consent language are unknown. No MoPH circular fixing a time limit or fee for records access was found.
- No statute-level general informed-consent rule found apart from Law 6/2025 (research) and the telemedicine circular. Treatment consent rules sit in facility policies and DHP guidance not retrieved here.

## 3. MoPH policies or circulars on health information: records retention, health information exchange, national EHR, data classification, sharing with third parties, data sharing policy

### Takeaway
The most concrete binding MoPH health-data instrument found is the **Qatar National Health Insurance Data Management Directive (v1.0, 20 Dec 2022)**. It requires in-country storage and processing, bans transfer of health insurance data outside Qatar, requires de-identification (anonymisation or pseudonymisation) for secondary use, makes HL7 FHIR the national exchange standard, and names the **Qatar Health Information Exchange Hub (QHIE-Hub)** as the future single gateway for exchanging patient information with MoPH. MoPH's eHealth Department (Emiri Decree No. 10 of 2016, Art. 14) is mandated to set standards on the confidentiality of personal health information. I found no published MoPH medical-records retention circular, general "health information privacy policy", or health-data classification policy.

### Cited Findings
- **National solutions:** MoPH is implementing the National Health Information Exchange (QHIE-Hub), the Qatar Health Insurance Solution (QHIS), and the Qatar Pharmaceutical Track and Trace System (QPTTS) — [QNHI Data Management Directive §1](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Scope:** the directive applies to "all entities (public and private) that store, use, process, and/or exchange health insurance data". It is meant to sit within cross-regulatory frameworks of MoPH, Qatar Central Bank, MCIT and the Ministry of Interior — [Directive §2](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Storage, §4:** under MoPH Decision No. 8 of 2022, health insurance data must be stored "in electronic systems or cloud infrastructure hosted inside the state of Qatar". Storage is governed by the data security and privacy guidelines of the competent cross-regulatory authorities, and entities must obtain patient consent for all stored PHI — [Directive §4](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Exchange, §7:** "HL7 FHIR is the National Standard for exchanging health information, including the claims submission". "QHIE-Hub will be the single gateway for exchanges of Patient's health information with MOPH, including health insurance claims as the end state (will be implemented in future phases)" — [Directive §7](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Cross-border, §8:** "It is prohibited to transfer health Insurance data outside the state of Qatar". MoPH may audit entities "storing, processing, or exchanging health and health insurance information" — [Directive §8](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **eHealth Department mandate, §9.2:** Emiri Decree No. 10 of 2016 (MoPH organisational structure), Art. 14, tasks the eHealth Department with "Proposing legislations and developing standards and regulations regarding the optimal usage of electronic environments to provide health services in the country, such as the confidentiality of personal health information and data, the method of storage and access to the system and coding". It also coordinates public providers' electronic systems so they can exchange information — [Directive §9.2](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Coding standards named in the directive's glossary:** ICD-10-AM, ICD-10-CM, ACHI, AR-DRG/IR-DRG, CPT, HCPCS, LOINC, SNOMED CT, CDT/SNODENT, Qatar National Drug Code (QNDC), and the Qatar National Clinical Coding Committee (QNCCC) — [Directive §11.1](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- MoPH runs a "Qatar National E-Health & Data Program (QNeDP)" under its National E-Health and Data Management Strategy — [MoPH QNeDP page](https://www.moph.gov.qa/english/strategies/Supporting-Strategies-and-Frameworks/NationalEHealthAndDataManagementStrategy/Pages/default.aspx) (title only; content not retrieved).
- MoPH's own app privacy policy says that "All personal information collected by MOPH is stored exclusively within Qatar" — [MoPH Privacy Policy](https://moph.gov.qa/english/Pages/Privacy-Policy.aspx) (via search snippet).
- **Retention [U]:** the only retention figure found is a private clinic's statement, attributed to MoPH requirements, of at least 10 years for adult records, and paediatric records until age 28 or 10 years, whichever is longer. No circular number is given — [Wellkins Medical Centre privacy policy](https://wellkins.com/privacy-policy/).

### Inferences
- The insurance-data directive formally covers "health insurance data", but hospitals generate that data (claims and remittances). Its in-country storage and processing rule, its ban on cross-border transfer, and its de-identification rule for secondary use are a strong signal of MoPH's general stance. An **external AI tool hosted outside Qatar** handling claim-linked or identifiable data would conflict with §§4, 5 and 8.
- Use "secondary use = de-identified (anonymised or pseudonymised) + licensed entity + restricted distribution" (§6) as the template for internal AI-development and analytics policy.

### Gaps
- No MoPH circular on medical-records retention or destruction periods was found. The 10-year figure is unverified.
- No public MoPH health-data classification policy or general "health information privacy" or "data sharing" policy was found. Facilities likely rely on the NCSA National Information Assurance (NIA) Policy and data classification standards, which are covered by other notes and were not verified here.
- Not confirmed: whether QHIE-Hub is now live for clinical (non-claims) exchange, and the EHR platforms in use (HMC and PHCC are commonly reported as Cerner and Sidra as Epic, but I found no source in this research).

## 4. Communicable/notifiable disease reporting: Decree-Law No. 17 of 1990 and amendments, notifiable lists, time limits, identified vs aggregate

### Takeaway
Decree-Law No. 17 of 1990 on the Prevention of Infectious (Communicable) Diseases, as amended by **Law No. 9 of 2020** (issued 26 March 2020), makes notification mandatory for every doctor who examines an infected or suspected case, as well as heads of institutions and employers. Breaches carry penalties of up to 3 years' imprisonment and QR 200,000. MoPH classes notifiable diseases as Class A (immediate, within 24 hours) and Class B (as soon as possible, up to 1 week; another study says 72 hours), across a list of about 78 diseases (2018 figure). Reports go electronically through MoPH's **SAVES** system with case-level epidemiological and clinical information, so reporting is identified, not aggregate. TB is notified to MoPH's Communicable Disease Control and Prevention Section.

### Cited Findings
- **Amendment:** the Amir issued Law No. 9 of 2020 amending provisions of Decree-Law No. 17 of 1990 on 26 March 2020, effective the day after publication in the Official Gazette — [Amiri Diwan, 26 Mar 2020](https://www.diwan.gov.qa/briefing-room/news/general/lg/2020/march/26/lg01?sc_lang=en); [Hukoomi](https://hukoomi.gov.qa/en/news/hh-the-amir-issues-law-amending-some-provisions-of-decree-law-on-prevention-of-infectious-diseases).
- **Who must notify [S]:** the amendment makes reporting mandatory for doctors, heads of institutions and employers — [Gulf Times, "Jail, fine for non-reporting of infectious diseases"](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/659327/Jail-fine-for-non-reporting-of-infectious-diseases). Art. 4 places the reporting duty on every doctor who examines an infected or suspected person, and also on the infected person, their guardian, and certain supervisors and employers — [Squire Patton Boggs Q&A, May 2020](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/en/insights/publications/2020/05/covid19-prevention-of-infectious-diseases-law-in-qatar-q-and-a) (search snippet; page now returns 404).
- **Article 2 (as amended by Law 9/2020)** is cited by MoPH as the basis for reporting duties — [The Peninsula, 5 Aug 2026](http://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/05/08/2026/moph-reminds-healthcare-facilities-to-report-food-poisoning-cases-within-24-hours).
- **Article 6 bis (new):** anyone placed in isolation or quarantine with the health authority's knowledge must stay in the designated place — [The Peninsula, 27 Mar 2020](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/27/03/2020/Amir-issues-law-amending-provisions-of-Prevention-of-Infectious-Diseases-Law).
- **Article 21 (penalties, as amended):** violating listed articles is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to QR 200,000. Violations of Arts. 15, 16 and 17 carry up to 1 month and/or QR 10,000 — [The Peninsula, 26 Mar 2020](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/26/03/2020/QR-200,000-fine,-imprisonment-for-violating-infectious-diseases-law).
- **Classes and time limits:** "Diseases that are immediately notifiable (within 24 hours) through telephone or fax are denoted as Class A diseases while those that may be notified as soon as possible (up to one week) are designated as Class B diseases". The list "encompassed 78 diseases" in 2018 — [Evaluation of the National Brucellosis Surveillance System in Qatar, 2018 (PMC6497511)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6497511/) (search snippet); [same study, Cureus 2019 PDF](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/18049/1556840667-20190502-22208-4nuaqe.pdf). **Conflicting [U]:** a Qatar hospital surveillance study calls the tiers "Group 1" (immediate, within 24 hrs, by telephone or fax) and "Group 2" ("as soon as possible but not immediately (72 hrs)"). That is 72 hours, not 1 week, for the second tier — [Qatar hospital surveillance study (PMC4197374)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4197374) (search snippet).
- **SAVES:** MoPH's electronic notification platform. Its stated goal is "to provide a platform where all national healthcare facilities, hospitals, and laboratories can notify decision-makers of infectious diseases" — [BMC Public Health 2024, "Enhancing the notification system for surveillance of infectious diseases" (Qatar)](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12889-024-18016-9) (search snippet).
- **Current practice, circular dated 4 Aug 2026:** all healthcare facilities and practitioners must report suspected or confirmed food-poisoning cases "within 24 hours of identification", submitting "all required epidemiological and clinical information through the SAVES platform" (MoPH's electronic reporting system). Non-compliance "may result in appropriate legal action". Legal basis cited: Decree-Law 17/1990 Art. 2 as amended by Law 9/2020, and Law No. 8 of 1990 on Human Food Control — [The Peninsula, 5 Aug 2026](http://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/05/08/2026/moph-reminds-healthcare-facilities-to-report-food-poisoning-cases-within-24-hours); [Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/247518/latest-news/health-ministry-mandates-prompt-reporting-of-food-poisoning-cases).
- **Tuberculosis [S]:** all TB cases are notified to MoPH's Communicable Disease Control and Prevention Section. A Qatar study found notification forms completed in only about 20% of confirmed-case records — [Qatar TB study (PMC4197374)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4197374) (search snippet; attribution between this and a PHCC clinical audit is uncertain — [PHCC TB clinical audit](https://www.gavinpublishers.com/article/view/clinical-audit-on-detection-of-tuberculosis-at-primary-health-care-corporation-qatar)).
- MoPH and WHO began preparing a **National Tuberculosis Plan 2026–2030** in June 2026 (incidence reduction, drug-resistant TB, preventive treatment). It is described as to be launched in the near future — [QNA, 21 Jun 2026](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-6/21/ministry-of-public-health-launches-preparations-for-national-tuberculosis-plan-2026-2030).
- Infectious-disease rules are a standard "obligated under the law" exception to medical confidentiality — [Holmes deck](https://qatar-weill.cornell.edu/Portals/15/Events/Sunanda's%20Presentation%20Nov.%204th%20Seminar.pdf?ver=2016-11-30-072548-353).

### Inferences
- Disclosure to MoPH for notifiable diseases is **legally mandated and identified, case-level**, so the hospital needs no patient consent. Data minimisation still applies: send what SAVES requires, not the full chart.
- An AI tool that flags possible notifiable diseases (e.g., TB on chest X-ray) does not discharge the doctor's own Art. 4 duty. Policy should route AI flags to clinician confirmation and SAVES reporting within the Class A/B windows.

### Gaps
- No current official MoPH notifiable-disease list (with class per disease and the TB class) was retrievable. The 78-disease, A/B structure is a 2018 figure. The second-tier deadline conflicts between sources (1 week vs 72 hours). TB's class and deadline remain unverified. Confirm with MoPH Health Protection & Communicable Disease Control or the SAVES user guidance.
- No official text of Decree-Law 17/1990 Arts. 2, 4 and 21 was seen. Whether the law contains an express confidentiality protection for notified data was not found.
- No source states whether any reporting is aggregate-only (e.g., for certain STIs/HIV) in Qatar.

## 5. Health insurance: Law No. 22 of 2021, its executive regulations, and what data insurers/TPAs may receive for claims

### Takeaway
Law No. 22 of 2021 (mandatory health insurance for expatriates and visitors) and MoPH Decision No. 8 of 2022 (Executive Regulations, in force 2 Sep 2022) bind insurers, brokers, claims management companies (TPAs), employers and providers to confidentiality. The Regulations require the member's **express written consent** before medical records are disclosed, including onward to reinsurers. Providers must supply claims information to insurers, TPAs and MoPH, but only through licensed, MoPH-registered entities. Claims and remittances must use the QNCC-approved Insurance Data Set (IDS v5.x) over HL7 FHIR, with ICD-10-AM/CM and related code sets. Data must stay in Qatar, and secondary uses must be de-identified. That secondary use explicitly includes **actuarial analysis and policy underwriting**. Insurers may not refuse compulsory cover. I found no express ban on using health data to discriminate in pricing.

### Cited Findings
- **Law:** Law No. 22 of 2021 regulating healthcare services within the State, announced 19 Oct 2021 — [Amiri Diwan](https://www.diwan.gov.qa/briefing-room/news/general/lg/2021/october/19/lg08?sc_lang=en). The law has 48 articles in six chapters, was published in the Official Gazette on 18 Oct 2021, takes effect six months after issuance, and repeals Law No. 7 of 1996 and Law No. 7 of 2013 — [Al Marzouqi Law commentary](https://malmarzouqilaw.com/commentary-on-law-no-22-of-2021-regulating-health-care-services/). Other sources give an effective date of 4 May 2022 and one gives 4 Nov 2021 as publication. **[U] conflicting dates** — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/qatar-issues-executive-regulations-to-the-new).
- **Executive Regulations:** Minister of Public Health Decision No. (8) of 2022, published in the Official Gazette 1 Sep 2022, in force 2 Sep 2022 — [Clyde & Co, Sept 2022](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/qatar-issues-executive-regulations-to-the-new); [Al Meezan (Arabic)](https://www.almeezan.qa/LawView.aspx?opt=&LawID=8980&language=ar).
- **Consent to disclose records:** the Regulations require "express written consent from each member to disclose their medical records". For reinsurance, the insurer needs each member's express written permission. Confidentiality duties extend to insurers, brokers, claims management companies, employers and other healthcare entities in the scheme. The Regulations do not explain how consent is to be obtained in practice — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/qatar-issues-executive-regulations-to-the-new).
- **Provider duties [S]:** keep accurate medical and financial records; supply information, data and documents to the competent department, insurers and claims management companies; give the competent department access to databases and records on request; protect beneficiaries' data — [Al Marzouqi Law commentary](https://malmarzouqilaw.com/commentary-on-law-no-22-of-2021-regulating-health-care-services/).
- **Insurer duties [S]:** insurers must not refuse any request for compulsory cover from an employer, recruiter or visitor without the competent department's written approval. They must pay out-of-network emergency care and maintain efficient systems — [Al Marzouqi Law commentary](https://malmarzouqilaw.com/commentary-on-law-no-22-of-2021-regulating-health-care-services/). Insurers must protect beneficiaries' information and give the competent department requested data, including medical files — [search summary of Law 22/2021 sources](https://www.qatarchamber.com/health-care-services-law/) (snippet; article numbers not verified).
- **Licensing:** mandatory cover must come from Qatar-licensed, MoPH-registered insurers. Claims management companies cannot take part unless authorised in Qatar and registered with MoPH — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/qatar-issues-executive-regulations-to-the-new).
- **Penalties [S]:** up to QAR 500,000 for private facilities refusing emergency or accident care, and for unregistered facilities. Up to QAR 30,000 per affected person for employers who fail to provide cover or deduct premiums. Conciliation is available except for the emergency-care offence (Art. 28) — [Al Marzouqi Law commentary](https://malmarzouqilaw.com/commentary-on-law-no-22-of-2021-regulating-health-care-services/). No specific penalty figure for confidentiality breach was found. Breach of confidentiality controls is listed as a violation in Decision 8/2022 — [Al Meezan (Arabic) snippet](https://www.almeezan.qa/LawView.aspx?opt=&LawID=8980&language=ar).
- **QNHI Data Management Directive (MoPH, v1.0, 20 Dec 2022):**
  - §5 Primary use = "all insurance data processing, claims management and adjudication activities performed by an insurance company or by a licensed claims processing entity on behalf of a licensed payor". Only registered and licensed entities may do it, on systems hosted in Qatar.
  - §6 Secondary use = "research, innovation, and public health policy making and decision support activities like actuarial analysis and policy underwriting". This must use "data de-identification technology to anonymize and pseudonymize" PHI, be done by licensed entities, and carry "restricted distribution of information and results".
  - §7 HL7 FHIR is mandatory for claims. "The Insurance Data Set (IDS v5.x), approved by QNCC, must be used as National Standard for Claims submission and remittance". A portal is provided for IDS Excel uploads by entities without electronic exchange.
  - §8 All entities storing, processing or exchanging health insurance data must register with MoPH. Brokers and consultants may not process or submit claims. Transfer outside Qatar is prohibited, and MoPH may audit.
  — all from [QNHI Data Management Directive](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).
- **Coding authority:** Amiri Decree No. (36) of 2022 gives MoPH's Health Finance and Insurance Department responsibility for "Preparing and applying the different coding systems of the health services, patient classification systems and the minimum data needed, and identifying the mechanisms of filing financial claims and the mechanisms of their arbitration and payment" — [Directive §9.1](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf). Contact for coding standards: MedicalCoding@moph.gov.qa (same source).

### Inferences
- **Claims disclosure (b)** is lawful when (i) the recipient is a Qatar-licensed, MoPH-registered insurer or TPA, (ii) the data is the IDS claim dataset (diagnosis and procedure codes) needed for adjudication, (iii) it is sent via FHIR or the MoPH portal and stays in Qatar, and (iv) for release of medical records beyond the claim, there is the member's express written consent. Hospital policy should separate "claim data set" from "medical record release" and collect consent at enrolment or admission.
- Brokers and consultants should get no claim-level PHI (Directive §8).
- The directive treats underwriting and actuarial analysis as secondary use that must be de-identified. That implies insurers should not use identifiable clinical data from claims to underwrite individuals. This is an inference, not an explicit anti-discrimination rule.

### Gaps
- Exact article numbers in Law 22/2021 and Decision 8/2022 for confidentiality, consent and data-supply duties were not verified (official text not machine-readable).
- No explicit rule found on medical-necessity review data limits, pre-existing-condition exclusions, or a ban on discrimination based on health data.
- Did not verify the phased rollout dates (e.g., visitors' insurance from early 2023) or any later amending decisions after 2022.

## 6. Research ethics: MoPH research regulations, IRB registration, use of patient data and de-identified data for research or AI development, secondary use

### Takeaway
Health research is now governed by statute: **Law No. 6 of 2025 regulating health research** (issued 25 March 2025; Official Gazette Issue 9, 13 April 2025). Its executive regulations came in **Cabinet Resolution No. 12 of 2026** (Official Gazette Issue 7 of 2026), along with Cabinet Decisions No. 13 of 2026 (National Committee for Health Research Ethics) and No. 14 of 2026 (Health Research Monitoring Committee). Research may only be done through a licensed research institution after approval by research review committees (IRBs), with informed consent. Biological samples cannot be exported without an MoPH permit (Art. 31). This statute layers on the older MoPH (ex-Supreme Council of Health) "Policies, Regulations and Guidelines for Research Involving Human Subjects", which require institutional Assurances and MoPH-registered IRBs. I did not find the specific secondary-use and de-identified-data rules in the 2025 law.

### Cited Findings
- **Law No. 6 of 2025:** issued by the Amir on 25 March 2025, effective on publication in the Official Gazette — [Amiri Diwan](https://www.diwan.gov.qa/en/briefing-room/news/general/lg/2025/march/25/lg01); [QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/news/news-details?id=hh-the-amir-issues-law-regulating-health-research&date=25%2F03%2F2025); [The Peninsula](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/25/03/2025/amir-issues-law-regulating-health-research); [Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/702524/qatar/amir-issues-law-on-health-research).
- Published in the Official Gazette Issue No. 9 of 2025, dated 13 April 2025. **Art. 31 [V, quoted]:** "is prohibited to export biological samples or any part taken from a living organism, including organs, tissues, cells, DNA, proteins, or others, outside the country for research purposes without first obtaining a permit from the competent department at the Ministry of Public Health, based on approval from the National Committee for Health Research Ethics". **Art. 32** establishes that Committee — [General Authority of Customs, Administrative Circular No. 41, 22 Jun 2025](https://www.customs.gov.qa/English/Legislations/Documents/Executive%20Resolutions/2025/Law%20No.%20(6)%20of%202025%20Regulating%20Health%20Research.pdf).
- **Scope [S]:** the law applies to all health research activities, research institutions, research review committees, researchers, participants and biobanks. Research may be conducted only through a research institution after approval from one or more research review committees. Reported protections include free research-related healthcare, limits on how often healthy volunteers join trials, and informed consent requirements — [Peninsula](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/25/03/2025/amir-issues-law-regulating-health-research); [QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/news/news-details?id=hh-the-amir-issues-law-regulating-health-research&date=25%2F03%2F2025).
- **Special conditions [S]:** the law sets detailed conditions for research involving gametes, surplus fertilised eggs, aborted or miscarried fetuses, children, persons lacking legal capacity, pregnant women, and embryonic stem cells — [Al Tamimi & Co., "New Regulatory Restrictions on Health Research in Qatar"](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update/the-healthcare-issue/articles/new-regulatory-restrictions-on-health-research-in-qatar/) (search snippet).
- **Cabinet Resolution No. 12 of 2026 (Executive Regulations), Official Gazette Issue 7 of 2026 [S]:**
  - covers clinical trials, laboratory/biomedical and genomic research, international collaborations, human biological samples, and biobanks;
  - institutions need a licence before research, valid 2 years and renewable, which may be amended, suspended or revoked;
  - every proposal needs scientific and ethical review, with consent forms, statistical plans, conflict-of-interest disclosures and participant-protection measures;
  - serious adverse events and "safety breaches" must be reported immediately;
  - violations include "failing to obtain informed consent", "Breaching confidentiality or biosafety rules" and unauthorised sample export, with sanctions of licence suspension or cancellation, project termination, and referral under the Health Research Law;
  - biobanks must file annual reports.
  — [Gulf Times, 11 May 2026](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/725301/qatar/research-regulations-set-to-enhance-ethics-safety-of-qatars-health-practices).
- **Cabinet Decision No. 13 of 2026** forms the National Committee for Health Research Ethics under MoPH (HMC as deputy chair) — [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/law/Qatar/CabinetDecision_13_2026/en). **Cabinet Decision No. 14 of 2026** creates the Health Research Monitoring Committee — [Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/725301/qatar/research-regulations-set-to-enhance-ethics-safety-of-qatars-health-practices).
- **Pre-2025 framework, still referenced by institutions:** the MoPH/SCH "Policies, Regulations and Guidelines for Research Involving Human Subjects" — [SCH policies PDF (WCM copy)](https://research.weill.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/sch_policiesandregulations.pdf). Institutions "engaged" in human-subjects research must hold an MoPH Assurance and run MoPH-registered IRBs — [Doha Institute research ethics](https://www.dohainstitute.edu.qa/en/research/pages/research-ethics-and-integrity.aspx); [Qatar University ethical compliance](https://www.qu.edu.qa/sites/en_US/research/offices/research-planning-development/research-excellence/ethical-compliance); [QU-IRB Handbook](https://www.qu.edu.qa/SiteImages/static_file/qu/research/documents/qu_irb/QU-IRB%20Handbook%20-%20Ethical%20Rules%20and%20Regulations.pdf). The MoPH research portal is research.moph.gov.qa — [DIFI Research Ethics Guide](https://difi.org.qa/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Research-Ethics-Guide-REG-.pdf).
- **De-identification for secondary use (insurance data):** secondary use for research and innovation must apply anonymisation or pseudonymisation and restricted distribution — [QNHI Directive §6](https://www.moph.gov.qa/_layouts/15/download.aspx?SourceUrl=%2FAdmin%2FLists%2FPublicationsAttachments%2FAttachments%2F226%2FQNHI-DATA-MANAGEMENT-ENG.pdf).

### Inferences
- Training or validating an AI model on hospital records is very likely "health research" under Law 6/2025 if done for generalisable knowledge. That means a licensed research institution, review-committee approval, consent or a committee-approved waiver, and confidentiality controls. The Executive Regulations make a confidentiality breach a research violation.
- Retrospective chart review and dataset release to an AI developer should go through IRB, with a de-identification plan modelled on QNHI §6. Exporting samples needs an MoPH permit under Art. 31; exporting *data* abroad is governed by the PDPPL and MoPH in-country hosting expectations.

### Gaps
- Full text of Law 6/2025 and Cabinet Resolution 12/2026 not seen, so the article numbers for consent, consent waiver, retrospective data use, de-identified data exemptions, data (as opposed to sample) export, and penalties are unknown.
- Whether the old SCH/MoPH human-subjects policies were formally superseded or retained alongside Law 6/2025 is not confirmed.

## 7. MoPH or national guidance specific to AI in healthcare (AI guidelines, AI as SaMD, digital health strategy, National Health Strategy 2024-2030)

### Takeaway
As of late 2026 I found **no binding MoPH regulation specific to healthcare AI** and **no published Qatari SaMD/AI medical-device framework**. The main health-specific document is the non-binding **"Research Guidelines for Healthcare AI Development" (HBKU, v1.0, 2025, with MoPH in an official advisory role)**. MoPH ran a 2024 national workshop on AI ethics in health research. The **National Health Strategy 2024–2030** names "digitization and data enablement" as a priority, including applied health intelligence and data privacy, security and ethics. National non-binding MCIT AI ethics principles (2024) and NCSA guidance apply across sectors.

### Cited Findings
- **HBKU Research Guidelines for Healthcare AI Development:** built 2021–2024 by a multidisciplinary HBKU team "with the MOPH undertaking an official advisory role". They "provide non-binding guidance to researchers who are developing AI systems in their healthcare-related research", covering ethics, data privacy and bias mitigation — [ResearchGate: Research Guidelines for Healthcare AI Development](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390763866_Research_Guidelines_for_Healthcare_AI_Development); [Solaiman et al., npj Digital Medicine 2025, "A True Lifecycle Approach towards governing healthcare AI"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01614-1). Eversheds lists them as v1.0 (2025), based on local legislation and international standards — [Eversheds Sutherland, "Mapping Qatar's Regulatory Landscape for AI", Nov 2025](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/poland/insights/mapping-qatars-regulatory-landscape-for-artificial-intelligence-november-2025).
- **MoPH National Health Research Ethics Workshop 2024** on AI in health research, held with NCSA, Qatar University and HBKU, to review regulations on health research and set ethical principles for AI use — [Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/152136/latest-news/moph-discusses-ethics-of-use-of-ai-in-health-research); [Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/695726/qatar/moph-discusses-ethics-of-ai-use-in-health-research); [The Peninsula, 2 Dec 2024](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/02/12/2024/moph-workshop-explores-developments-in-use-of-ai-in-health-research).
- **Cross-sector AI instruments (non-binding)** per Eversheds:
  - MCIT Principles for Ethical Development and Deployment of AI (2024): "do no harm", plus risk assessments for safety, privacy and bias;
  - MCIT Principles for Ethical Use of AI (2024): human oversight;
  - NCSA PDPPL Guidance Hub (2025), covering privacy by design, breach notification, "special category processing" and international transfers;
  - NCSA National Information Security Compliance Framework (NISCF) Audit Standard v3.0 (2024), with AI "requirements for accuracy, reliability, and validation of AI-generated results" (Section A.C.E.3.2);
  - Cybercrime Prevention Law No. 14 of 2014, which may cover model theft, data poisoning or unauthorised API access;
  - forthcoming items: MCIT National AI Policy, Regulatory Sandbox Policy, and a draft National Center for AI decision (Sept 2025).
  — [Eversheds Sutherland, Nov 2025](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/poland/insights/mapping-qatars-regulatory-landscape-for-artificial-intelligence-november-2025).
- Qatar has no centralised AI statute; sector rules mix the national AI strategy with general administrative rules — [regulations.ai Qatar summary](https://regulations.ai/regulations/qatar-summary); [Eversheds](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/poland/insights/mapping-qatars-regulatory-landscape-for-artificial-intelligence-november-2025).
- **Conflicting claim [U]:** a 2026 review says MoPH, with the Communications Regulatory Authority, uses a "risk-tiered system" to regulate healthcare AI. I found no official document to support it — [Frontiers in AI, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1702242/full).
- **National Health Strategy 2024–2030:** launched by the Prime Minister, Qatar's third NHS — [Government Communications Office](https://www.gco.gov.qa/en/top-news/prime-minister-launches-national-health-strategy-2024-2030/); [NHS 2024-2030 PDF](https://www.moph.gov.qa/english/NHS/Documents/NHS_2024_2030_EN.pdf). The MoPH priorities page identifies weaknesses: inconsistent data, low satisfaction with digital platforms, no unified data lake, low AI adoption. It lists "applied health intelligence" and "data privacy, security and ethics" under "Digitization and data enablement" — [MoPH NHS Priorities](https://moph.gov.qa/english/NHS/Pages/Priorities.aspx) (via search snippet); [MoPH NHS Aspiration](https://moph.gov.qa:443/english/NHS/Pages/The-Aspiration.aspx).
- **Medical devices / SaMD:** MoPH's Pharmacy and Drug Control Department is described as the sole device regulator, requiring a local authorised representative. Implantables need pre-market registration and other devices need import permits per shipment. This is a consultancy source; no SaMD- or AI-specific rules were found — [OMC Medical, Qatar Medical Device Registration](https://omcmedical.com/qatar-medical-device-registration).

### Inferences
- With no AI-specific health regulation, an **external AI diagnostic tool** is governed by the general stack: medical confidentiality (Law 2/1983 Art. 20; Penal Code Art. 332), the PDPPL, MoPH in-country hosting expectations (QNHI Directive), NCSA/NIA security controls, DHP practitioner accountability, and, if used for development or validation, Law 6/2025. Hospital policy should demand in-Qatar hosting or a lawful transfer basis, a contract with no secondary use by the vendor, de-identification where feasible, clinician-in-the-loop, and validation evidence (aligned with the NISCF A.C.E.3.2 expectation).
- Whether the AI tool is a regulated medical device is unclear. Policy should require evidence of foreign approval (CE/FDA) and check with PDCD.

### Gaps
- No official MoPH AI policy, circular or SaMD guidance was found. The full HBKU guidelines and the NHS 2024–2030 digital chapter were not read.
- National Health Strategy targets and KPIs for AI and health information exchange were not extracted.

## 8. Telemedicine / digital health guidelines that touch data handling

### Takeaway
MoPH's DHP has approved telemedicine (circular reported as 2024/04). It requires Qatar-licensed practitioners and informed consent covering remote-care risks. A 2022 teleradiology circular treats confidentiality as "paramount": reporting radiologists must sign a patient-confidentiality form, and DHP audits regularly. Detailed telehealth data-security codes were not found.

### Cited Findings
- DHP states "Telemedicine services are acceptable and approved by the DHP/MOPH". Practitioners must hold Qatari licensure tied to an employer, and patients "must provide informed consent understanding the limitations and potential risks associated with remote care" — [DHP circular (Eng)](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa/en/QCHPCirculars/Eng-Circular.pdf) (via search snippet; number 2024/04 per the snippet, not verified).
- Teleradiology, Circular 6/2022: "Patient safety and confidentiality are paramount There should be regular audit of this by DHP"; "The reporting radiologist should sign a patient confidentiality form Plus conflict-of-interest declaration" — [DHP Circular (6-2022) Eng](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa//en/QCHPCirculars/Circular%20(6-2022)-Eng.pdf); [Arabic version](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa//en/QCHPCircularsAR/Circular%20(6-2022)-Arb.pdf) (via search snippet).
- An earlier DLA Piper handbook answered "No" to whether Qatar had codes of conduct on telehealth systems or telehealth data security. This may be outdated — [DLA Piper Telehealth Around the World, Qatar](https://www.dlapiperintelligence.com/telehealth/countries/handbook.pdf?c=QA).
- A COVID-era DHP circular, 07/2020, also addresses remote practice — [DHP Circular (07-2020) Eng](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa//en/QCHPCirculars/Circular%20(07-2020)%20-%20Eng.pdf) (content not retrieved).

### Inferences
- The teleradiology model (confidentiality undertaking, conflict-of-interest declaration, DHP audit) is a ready template for **remote or external reading by an AI vendor or outsourced clinicians**. Hospitals can require the same undertakings and audit rights in AI-vendor contracts.

### Gaps
- Full telemedicine circular text not retrieved. Its data-storage, recording and cross-border provisions are unknown.
